FEMALE, 31 YEARS OLD
Medical history:
- Pathological proximate: The patient presented with pain and swelling in her left calf, which had started approximately 3 days ago and had been progressively worsening. She reported no shortness of breath, chest pain or haemoptysis. She reported no fever. She reported no recent trauma or prolonged immobilisation.
- Remote pathology: No known medical conditions. No history of thromboembolic events or family history of thrombophilia. Currently taking oral oestrogen-progestogen (contraceptive pill). Has smoked approximately one packet a day for 10 years. Denies any drug allergies.

Objective examination: The patient is alert, oriented and cooperative. She is not in distress at rest. Vesicular breath sounds are present in all lung fields; no additional sounds. Heart sounds are normal-rate, rhythmic, with clear intervals. Abdomen soft to palpation, not tender. Left lower limb: oedema of the calf, skin warm and erythematous distally. Right lower limb normal. BP 122/76 mmHg. Heart Rate 82 bpm. SpO₂ 99% on air. Body Temperature 36.8°C.

Clinical diary:
• Wells score for DVT calculated: tenderness along the deep venous course (+1), unilateral oedema (+1), swelling >3 cm (+1). Total score: 3 — high pretest probability.
• Venous access established and D-dimer, biochemistry, complete blood count and coagulation tests performed.
• ECG performed: sinus rhythm, no abnormalities.
• Arterial blood gas analysis performed: normal gas exchange, no acid-base abnormalities. Performed to complete the diagnostic work-up in view of the suspected thromboembolism; results within normal limits.
• Test results reviewed: D-dimer 2,840 ng/mL (normal range <500), significantly elevated. No signs of inflammation. Complete blood count and coagulation parameters within normal limits.
• A venous Doppler ultrasound of the left lower limb was performed: deep vein thrombosis of the left gastrocnemius veins, not extending to the popliteal vein. No involvement of the proximal venous circulation.
• The on-call angiologist/internist was contacted for a specialist consultation and to determine the treatment plan.
• Anticoagulant therapy was initiated: enoxaparin 8,000 IU (100 IU/kg, weight 80 kg) subcutaneously, with the first dose administered in A&E.

Diagnosis: deep vein thrombosis of the left gastrocnemius veins
Prognosis: 4 days
