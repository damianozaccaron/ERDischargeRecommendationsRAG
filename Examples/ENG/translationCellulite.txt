Male, 47 years old
Medical history:
- Pathological proximate: The patient presents with swelling, redness and warmth of the left leg that began approximately 5 days ago, progressively worsening, associated with high fever (max 39.4°C at home) and chills. He also reports marked asthenia and loss of appetite. He denies cough, dyspnoea or chest pain. He does not report recent trauma to the leg.
- Remote pathology: Homeless patient. Reports intravenous use of narcotic substances (heroin), active. No known formally diagnosed conditions. Does not take regular home therapy. No known declared drug allergies. 

Objective examination: Patient alert, oriented, poorly cooperative. Poor general condition. Markedly dehydrated, malnourished. Severely deficient personal hygiene. Vesicular breath sounds present in all lung fields, no added sounds. Heart sounds tachycardic, rhythmic. On cardiac auscultation a 3/6 holosystolic murmur is appreciated at the mid-precordium, radiating towards the axilla, not reported in the medical history nor documented in previous visits. Soft abdomen, non-tender. CVA Tenderness and Blumberg negative. Left lower limb: erythematous plaque with sharp and irregular margins extending from the ankle to the middle third of the leg, skin warm, oedematous, tender onpalpation. Multiple venipuncture scars present along the veins of the forearm bilaterally and of the left popliteal fossa. No fluctuation suggestive of superficial abscess. No contralateral dependent oedema. BP 98/62 mmHg. Heart Rate 118 bpm. SpO2 96% RA. Body Temperature 39.1°C.

Clinical diary:
• Venous access obtained with difficulty due to compromised venous access; blood cultures x2 taken from distinct sites before any antibiotic therapy. Biochemistry, complete blood count, inflammatory markers, procalcitonin, coagulation, renal and liver function, serologies for HIV, HBV, HCV performed.
• ECG performed: sinus tachycardia, Heart Rate 118 bpm, no ST segment changes, no signs of conduction disturbances.
• Arterial blood gas performed: mild hypoxaemia (PaO2 68 mmHg), compensatory respiratory alkalosis, lactate 2.8 mmol/L (mildly elevated).
• Infusion of normal saline 500cc as a bolus started for relative hypotension, with improvement of BP to 108/68 mmHg. Continuous monitoring of vital signs started.
• Tests reviewed: marked neutrophilic leukocytosis (WBC 22.400/μL, N 89%). C-reactive protein 28.4 mg/dL. Procalcitonin 18.2 ng/mL (n.v. <0.5), markedly elevated, suggestive of systemic bacterial infectious process. Creatinine 1.6 mg/dL (mild renal failure). AST/ALT mildly elevated. Coagulation: PT and aPTT at upper limits. HIV negative, HCV positive, HBsAg negative.
• Bedside ultrasound performed: no pericardial effusion. No diffuse B-lines. Left ventricular systolic function visually preserved. On examination of the right chambers a mobile hyperechoic image is appreciated at the level of the tricuspid valve, suspicious for vegetation. No significant pleural effusion.
• Considering the overall picture — erysipelas/cellulitis in IVDU patient, high fever, markedly elevated procalcitonin, increased lactate, newly detected heart murmur and suspicious echocardiographic finding — the picture is strongly suggestive of infective endocarditis with likely cutaneous/venipuncture portal of entry. The on-call infectious disease specialist is urgently contacted. 
• Empirical broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy started after blood cultures were taken: vancomycin 25mg/kg as slow infusion (estimated weight 65kg, dose 1.625mg rounded to 1.500mg) for coverage of methicillin-resistant staphylococcus (MRSA), priority pathogen in IVDU patient.
• The infectious disease specialist assesses the patient in the ED and confirms the indication for urgent hospitalisation for dedicated transthoracic/transoesophageal echocardiography, monitoring of blood cultures and continuation of targeted antibiotic therapy.

Diagnosis: ERYSIPELAS/CELLULITIS OF THE LEFT LOWER LIMB — SUSPECTED INFECTIVE ENDOCARDITIS (likely on tricuspid valve) IN IVDU PATIENT
Prognosis: urgent hospitalisation
