Female, 28 years old
Medical history: 
- Pathological proximate: The patient presents with abdominal pain that began approximately 12 hours before arrival, initially diffuse and cramp-like, progressively migrated and localised in the right iliac fossa. Associated nausea with one episode of vomiting. Reports evening low-grade fever in the last 24 hours (Body Temperature max 37.8°C at home). Denies diarrhoea or changes in bowel habits. Last menstruation approximately 2 weeks ago, regular cycle.
- Remote pathology: No known pathologies. No previous surgical procedures. Was not taking home pharmacological therapy. Denies drug allergies.

Objective examination: Patient alert, oriented, cooperative. In distress. Vesicular breath sounds present in all lung fields, no added sounds. Heart sounds normal-rate, rhythmic, no murmurs. Soft abdomen, markedly painful on palpation in the right iliac fossa. Blumberg positive. Rovsing's sign positive. CVA Tenderness negative. Cystic points non-tender. No dependent oedema. BP 118/74 mmHg. Heart Rate 102 bpm. SpO2 99% RA. Body Temperature 38.1°C.

Clinical diary:
• Venous access obtained and execution of biochemistry, complete blood count, inflammatory markers, urine pregnancy test (urinary β-hCG), urinalysis.
• ECG performed: sinus rhythm, reactive sinus tachycardia, no ST segment changes.
• Tests reviewed: neutrophilic leukocytosis (WBC 14.200/μL, N 84%). C-reactive protein 4.2 mg/dL. β-hCG negative. Urinalysis: within normal limits, excludes acute urological pathology.
• Ketorolac 30mg in 100cc saline solution administered for analgesia and ondansetron 4mg IV for nausea, with partial benefit.
• Bedside ultrasound performed: no appreciable free intra-abdominal effusion. No hydronephrosis bilaterally. Appendix not visualisable with this method due to gas interposition.
• Considering the clinical picture, the laboratory findings and the positivity of the appendiceal signs, the patient is sent to radiology for abdominal CT and pelvis with contrast medium.
• CT report: Finding compatible with non-perforated acute appendicitis.
• The on-call general surgery is contacted for specialist consultation. The surgeon examines the patient and confirms the surgical indication.
• The patient is informed of the diagnosis and of the need for surgery. Informed consent obtained. Fasting is started and the patient is prepared for the operating theatre.

Diagnosis: ACUTE APPENDICITIS
Prognosis: hospitalisation
