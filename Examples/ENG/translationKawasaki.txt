Male, 2 years old and 8 months
Medical history: 
- Pathological proximate: The patient is brought to the ED by his parents for high fever persisting for 6 days (Body Temperature max 40.1°C), not responsive to home antipyretic therapy (paracetamol and ibuprofen at weight-appropriate doses). The parents report onset, in the last 2-3 days, of redness of the eyes without discharge, skin rash on the trunk, cracked and reddened lips, and unusual-looking tongue. They also report right laterocervical swelling. The child is irritable, refuses play and feeding. The mother denies episodes of cough, rhinorrhoea or diarrhoea. No known contact with sick individuals.
Remote pathology: Born at term, eutocic delivery, normal psychomotor development. Vaccinations up to date for age. No known conditions. No previous ED visits. No ongoing home therapy. The mother denies known drug allergies.

Objective examination: Patient alert, irritable, crying during the assessment. General condition compromised. Vesicular breath sounds present in all lung fields, no added sounds. Heart sounds tachycardic, rhythmic, no murmurs. No appreciable murmurs. Soft abdomen, mildly tender on diffuse palpation, no evident organomegaly.

Clinical diary:
• Venous access obtained and biochemistry, complete blood count, inflammatory markers, procalcitonin, ESR, fibrinogen, ALT/AST, bilirubin, albumin, sodium, potassium, renal function, urinalysis performed. Blood cultures x2 are performed before any antibiotic therapy, to rule out bacteraemia. 
• ECG performed: sinus tachycardia, Heart Rate 138 bpm, no ST segment changes, no conduction disturbances, PR interval normal for age.
• Tests reviewed: neutrophilic leukocytosis (WBC 18.600/μL, N 78%). C-reactive protein 14.2 mg/dL. ESR 88 mm/h. Fibrinogen 620 mg/dL (elevated). Procalcitonin 2.4 ng/mL. Platelets
498.000/μL (thrombocytosis). ALT mildly elevated. Albumin 2.8 g/dL (hypoalbuminaemia). Urinalysis: sterile leukocyturia. Sodium 132 mEq/L (mild hyponatraemia).
• The on-call paediatrician is urgently contacted for specialist consultation. The clinical picture persistent fever for more than 5 days associated with all 5 clinical criteria of Kawasaki disease is diagnostic for Kawasaki disease in classic form.
• Clinical criteria found: — Fever for 6 days (Body Temperature 39.8°C in the ED) — Bilateral non-exudative conjunctivitis — Changes of the lips and oral cavity: erythematous and cracked lips, strawberry tongue — Polymorphous exanthem on the trunk and at the root of the limbs — Right cervical lymphadenopathy with single lymph node of approximately 2cm, tender, non-fluctuant — Erythema andmild oedema of the hands and feet BP 96/58 mmHg. Heart Rate 138 bpm. SpO2 99% RA. Body Temperature 39.8°C. Weight 14kg.
• Bedside ultrasound performed by the paediatrician: cardiac function visually preserved, coronary arteries not clearly dilated with this method; dedicated echocardiography reserved for the hospitalisation setting.

• The family is informed of the diagnosis, of the need for urgent hospitalisation and of the importance of early treatment to reduce the risk of coronary aneurysms.

Diagnosis: KAWASAKI DISEASE — CLASSIC FORM (5/5 criteria present)
