Male, 71 years old
Medical history:
Pathological proximate: The patient presents with palpitations and a sensation of irregular heartbeat that began approximately 4 hours before presentation, associated with mild asthenia. Denies chest pain, syncope or pre-syncope. Reports a similar, self-resolved episode approximately 3 months ago, not investigated. Denies dyspnoea.
Remote pathology: Hypertension on therapy with amlodipine 5mg. Diabetic on therapy with metfromin. No known dyslipidaemia. Ex-smoker, approximately 1 pack/day for 30 years, STOPPED fifteen years ago. No known cardiovascular events nor family history of arrhythmias. Denies drug allergies.

Objective examination: Patient alert, oriented, cooperative. Mildly anxious. Eupneic at rest. Vesicular breath sounds present in all lung fields, no added sounds. Arrhythmic heart sounds, murmurs present, heart rate approximately 110-120 bpm. No appreciable murmurs. Soft abdomen, non-tender. CVA Tenderness and Blumberg negative. No dependent oedema or signs of deep vein thrombosis. BP 158/94 mmHg. Heart Rate 114 bpm. SpO2 97% RA. Body Temperature 36.5°C.

Clinical diary:
• Immediate ECG performed: atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response (Heart Rate 114 bpm), no signs of acute ischaemia, no repolarisation abnormalities. First documented finding.
• Venous access obtained and troponin, biochemistry, complete blood count, TSH, pro-BNP, electrolytes performed. Arterial blood gas performed: physiological exchange, mild hypoxaemia (PaO2 72 mmHg).
• The patient is sent to radiology for 2-view chest X-ray: mild increase in hilar vascular markings bilaterally, no frank pleural effusion, no parenchymal consolidations. Moderate enlargement of the cardiac silhouette.
• Tests reviewed: troponin <14, negative. No significant inflammatory markers. Complete blood count within normal limits. TSH within normal limits. Pro-BNP 480 pg/mL (mildly elevated). Potassium 3.8 mEq/L. Sodium 139 mEq/L.
• Bedside ultrasound performed: no pericardial effusion. Modest left atrial dilatation. No diffuse B-lines in the lung fields. Left ventricular systolic function visually preserved.
• The on-call cardiology team is contacted for specialist consultation. Considering the duration of the episode reported by the patient (approximately 4 hours), the approach is discussed: the patient is haemodynamically stable, therefore rate control is chosen in the first instance.
• Metoprolol tartrate 5mg IV administered under continuous monitoring. ECG check after 20 minutes: persistent AF, Heart Rate reduced to 88 bpm. Patient reports mild improvement in symptoms.
• The patient is informed of the diagnosis, of the need for anticoagulant therapy and of the related thromboembolic risk. CHA₂DS₂-VASc calculated: 3 points ( according to the ESC 2020 guidelines). Indication for oral anticoagulant therapy.
• The patient is admitted to cardiology for diagnostic work-up, initiation of anticoagulant therapy and elective evaluation of rhythm vs rate strategy.

Diagnosis: NEWLY DIAGNOSED ATRIAL FIBRILLATION WITH RAPID VENTRICULAR RESPONSE

Prognosis: hospitalisation
